# 1.5 Understanding External Influences on a Business

## Chapter Summary

Businesses are affected by people and organisations around them, as well as wider external forces they cannot control. Stakeholders such as owners, employees, customers, suppliers, banks, government, local communities and pressure groups all have different objectives, which can create conflict.

Technology, legislation and the economy strongly influence business decisions. E-commerce, social media, digital communication and payment systems create opportunities but also increase costs, competition and security risks. Laws protect consumers and employees, while economic factors such as employment, consumer spending, inflation, tax, interest rates and exchange rates affect costs, demand and profitability.

## Key Concepts

- **Stakeholder**: anyone with an interest in the activities and decisions of a business.
- **Stakeholder conflict**: disagreement caused by different stakeholder objectives.
- **E-commerce**: buying and selling goods or services online.
- **Social media**: online platforms used for communication, promotion, recruitment and customer feedback.
- **Digital communication**: electronic communication such as email, mobile messaging and video conferencing.
- **Consumer law**: legislation protecting customers from unfair or unsafe business behaviour.
- **Employment law**: legislation protecting workers' pay, rights, equality and safety.
- **Inflation**: a sustained rise in prices.
- **Interest rate**: the cost of borrowing and reward for saving.
- **Exchange rate**: the value of one currency compared with another.
- **SPICED**: strong pound means imports cheaper and exports dearer.

## Key Arguments

- Stakeholders affect business decisions, and business decisions affect stakeholders.
- Objectives can conflict: for example, higher wages help employees but raise business costs.
- Technology can improve efficiency, sales and communication, but businesses must manage cost, reliability, competition and security risks.
- Legislation increases compliance costs but protects reputation and avoids fines.
- Economic changes affect both business costs and customer demand.
- External influences are outside business control, so successful firms monitor change and prepare responses.

## Examples From the Chapter

- **Relocation decision**: may help customers and owners but harm employees and the old local community.
- **Employee pay demands**: can increase costs and force decisions about prices or profit margins.
- **E-commerce**: allows 24/7 selling and wider markets, but increases online competition and distribution challenges.
- **LinkedIn**: used as a recruitment and CV-sharing platform.
- **PayPal, Apple Pay and Android Pay**: examples of technology-based payment systems.
- **Consumer Rights Act 2015**: requires goods to match description, be satisfactory quality and fit for purpose.
- **Health and Safety at Work Act 1974**: requires employers to protect employees and others affected by business activity.
- **Hotel example**: shows how tax changes, anti-pollution laws and mobile booking trends can force price, staffing and technology decisions.

## Action Points / Practical Takeaways

- Identify key stakeholders before major decisions.
- Consider both short-term and long-term effects on each stakeholder group.
- Use technology where it improves customer service, efficiency or reach, but budget for setup, training and security.
- Follow consumer, employment and health and safety law to avoid fines and reputational damage.
- Monitor economic indicators such as income, inflation, interest rates and exchange rates.
- Prepare flexible plans for changes outside the business's control.
- Remember SPICED when analysing a strong pound: imports cheaper, exports dearer.
