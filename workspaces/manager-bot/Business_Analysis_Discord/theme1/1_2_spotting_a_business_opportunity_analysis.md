# 1.2 Spotting a Business Opportunity

## Chapter Summary

Successful businesses understand customer needs before launching or changing products. Customers usually care about quality, price, choice and convenience, and businesses must design their offer around those needs.

Market research helps entrepreneurs reduce risk by collecting information about customers, competitors, demand, market size and market segments. Research can be primary or secondary, qualitative or quantitative, and may use social media. Segmentation and market mapping help a business choose a target market and identify possible gaps, but all research must be checked for reliability.

## Key Concepts

- **Customer needs**: what customers require from a product, including price, quality, choice and convenience.
- **Market research**: gathering information to help business decisions and reduce risk.
- **Primary research**: new research collected first-hand for a specific purpose.
- **Secondary research**: existing information collected for another purpose.
- **Qualitative research**: opinions, attitudes and reasons behind behaviour.
- **Quantitative research**: numerical data, usually from larger samples.
- **Market segmentation**: dividing a market into groups with similar needs.
- **Market map**: a diagram showing product positions using two important dimensions, such as price and quality.
- **Direct competition**: businesses selling very similar products.
- **Indirect competition**: alternatives that satisfy a similar customer need.

## Key Arguments

- A business must be customer focused because competition gives customers choice.
- Market research reduces risk, but it is never perfectly accurate.
- Small businesses must balance useful research with limited time and funds.
- Segmentation helps target promotion and product design more effectively.
- A gap in a market map is only valuable if real customer demand exists.
- Competitors affect decisions about price, product features, location and quality.

## Examples From the Chapter

- **Rush-hour trains**: show how price can change according to timing and demand.
- **Online shopping and home delivery**: show the rising importance of convenience.
- **Observation outside a shop**: can show footfall, window interest and buying behaviour.
- **TripAdvisor and Amazon reviews**: provide secondary data about customer opinions.
- **Divine Chocolate and Green & Black's**: positioned themselves as high-quality, premium chocolate brands.
- **Local cafes and hairdressers**: may differentiate through service, location or customer segment.

## Action Points / Practical Takeaways

- Define the target customer before deciding product, price, place or promotion.
- Use both primary and secondary research where possible.
- Check research for bias, age, sample size and relevance.
- Use social media for fast feedback, but do not assume it represents all customers.
- Segment the market to focus resources on the most attractive customer group.
- Study direct and indirect competitors before choosing price, quality and location.
