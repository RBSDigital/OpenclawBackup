Daily UK Cyber-Fraud and Identity-Fraud Awareness Report

Reference: 6 November 2026, 09:00 UTC
Audience: UK individuals and organisations
Prepared by: Ada

Executive summary

The strongest current signal is the scale of impersonation-led phishing across email, text, calls, websites, adverts and QR codes. The NCSC reports more than 59.6 million scam reports and 263,700 scams removed across 466,300 URLs as of September 2026. The NCA identifies investment, romance, courier and payment-diversion fraud as priority UK threats, and says 67% of fraud reported in the UK is estimated to be cyber-enabled. The NCA also says card-not-present fraud is increasing.

Immediate rule: stop, break contact, and verify through a separately sourced official channel. Never use a link, phone number, QR code or bank details supplied by the approach. If money or account access may be at risk, contact the bank or payment provider immediately.

Ranking method and limitations

This is a defensive prioritisation, not a precise national league table. Ranking uses 40% current UK volume/prevalence where measured, 30% current loss/harm or priority warnings, 20% operational activity in current official guidance/disruption, and 10% breadth across people and organisations. Sources use different definitions, reporting channels and windows; fraud is under-reported. Related delivery channels are deduplicated into one canonical theme unless evidence or response is materially different.

Ranked threats

1Mass impersonation phishing across email, SMS, calls, websites, adverts and QR codes [UK-PHISHING-IMPERSONATION]

What it is: Criminals impersonate trusted organisations to steal credentials, personal data or money; email, smishing and vishing are one theme.

Warning signs: Unexpected contact; authority, urgency, fear or scarcity; pressure to click, scan, pay or verify; look-alike domains; requests for passwords or codes.

Guidance and reporting: NCSC spot-scam guidance. Report suspicious email to report@phishing.gov.uk; texts/call numbers to 7726; crime to Report Fraud in E/W/NI or Police Scotland 101.

Evidence/date: NCSC reports >59.6m scam reports and 263.7k scams removed across 466,300 URLs as of Sep 2026. Guidance published 26 Nov 2021, reviewed 5 Sep 2022; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

2Investment and cryptocurrency fraud [UK-INVESTMENT-CRYPTO]

What it is: Fake investments, trading platforms, social-media promotions and cloned financial firms persuade victims to send money to fictitious schemes.

Warning signs: Guaranteed or extraordinary returns; unsolicited contact; exclusivity; urgency or secrecy; crypto transfers; firm cannot be verified on FCA Firm Checker.

Guidance and reporting: FCA protect-yourself and fake-communications guidance. FCA 0800 111 6768 for FCA concerns; otherwise Report Fraud/Police Scotland and the bank.

Evidence/date: NCA identifies investment fraud as a UK priority and reports losses above £100,000 per hour. FCA page updated 19 Jan 2026; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

3Romance and relationship fraud [UK-ROMANCE-FRAUD]

What it is: A criminal builds an online relationship and requests money, gifts, account access or help with an emergency or investment.

Warning signs: Rapid intimacy; excuses to avoid verification; secrecy; repeated emergencies; requests to receive or move money.

Guidance and reporting: Stop! Think Fraud protection guidance. Tell the bank; report to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101 and the platform.

Evidence/date: NCA lists romance fraud among UK priority frauds. Stop! Think Fraud cites nearly 3m adult victims in England and Wales in year ending June 2024; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

4Payment diversion and business email compromise [UK-PAYMENT-DIVERSION-BEC]

What it is: A criminal impersonates a supplier, colleague, solicitor or customer, or compromises an account, to redirect an invoice, payroll or payment.

Warning signs: Changed bank details; urgency or confidentiality; bypassed approvals; look-alike address or compromised thread.

Guidance and reporting: NCSC online-services guidance. Pause payment and independently verify; notify bank, security team and police route.

Evidence/date: NCA identifies payment diversion as a UK priority. NCSC small-organisation guidance published/reviewed 11 Jan 2024; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

5Courier fraud and bank-account impersonation [UK-COURIER-BANK-IMPERSONATION]

What it is: A caller impersonates a bank, police officer, regulator or courier and seeks cards, cash, transfers or codes.

Warning signs: Move money for safety; PIN/one-time-code request; courier collecting cards or cash; pressure to stay on the line.

Guidance and reporting: NCSC scam-sign guidance. Hang up and call the bank using the card or 159; report crime to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Evidence/date: NCA identifies courier fraud as a UK priority; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

6Card-not-present and online payment fraud [UK-CARD-NOT-PRESENT]

What it is: Stolen card details or payment credentials are used for purchases without the physical card.

Warning signs: Unrecognised transactions; unexpected verification; pressure to disclose card details or codes; unverifiable merchant/site.

Guidance and reporting: NCSC personal-information guidance. Contact card issuer immediately via a known channel and report suspected fraud.

Evidence/date: NCA says card-not-present fraud is increasing; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

7Account takeover and credential theft [UK-ACCOUNT-TAKEOVER]

What it is: Stolen passwords, sessions or codes are used to take over email, social, cloud, shopping or financial accounts.

Warning signs: Unexpected reset alerts; unfamiliar login/recovery details; unsolicited 2SV prompts; strange messages sent from your account.

Guidance and reporting: NCSC online-services guidance. Use official recovery, change reused passwords, enable 2SV/passkeys, notify bank and report crime.

Evidence/date: Supported by current NCSC phishing and online-service guidance; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

8Identity theft and new-account fraud [UK-IDENTITY-THEFT]

What it is: Personal information is reused to impersonate someone, obtain services, open accounts or support another fraud.

Warning signs: Unexpected credit searches, bills, accounts or benefit correspondence; missing post; unknown authentication requests.

Guidance and reporting: NCSC personal-information guidance. Contact providers and bank, check credit files, consider Cifas protective registration and report crime.

Evidence/date: Current guidance confirms later reuse of stolen personal information. Cifas trend pages unavailable, so no Cifas volume claim; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

9Government, tax, benefits and public-service impersonation [UK-PUBLIC-SERVICE-IMPERSONATION]

What it is: Fake messages or calls imitate HMRC, DVLA, NHS, police, courts or other public bodies to demand payment or identity data.

Warning signs: Fines or refunds; arrest/cancellation threats; requests for identity, bank data or codes; non-official links.

Guidance and reporting: NCSC scam-sign guidance. Use the department's official route; email report@phishing.gov.uk, texts/calls 7726, losses Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Evidence/date: NCSC explicitly identifies government impersonation as an authority tactic; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

10Online shopping and marketplace non-delivery fraud [UK-ONLINE-PURCHASE-FRAUD]

What it is: Fake shops, counterfeit goods, non-delivery listings or deceptive marketplace transactions take payment without supplying the item.

Warning signs: Unusually low price; pressure to pay outside platform; no credible business details; refusal of protected payment.

Guidance and reporting: Stop! Think Fraud and NCSC checks. Contact payment provider and marketplace; fake adverts to ASA; losses through police route.

Evidence/date: Current guidance covers websites and apps; comparable current volume unavailable; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

11Advance-fee loan, grant and benefit fraud [UK-ADVANCE-FEE-FINANCIAL]

What it is: A promised loan, grant, benefit, prize or financial service is used to obtain an upfront fee or identity/banking information.

Warning signs: Guaranteed approval; processing/release fee; remote-access or document request; urgency and secrecy.

Guidance and reporting: FCA financial-scam guidance. FCA if claimed regulated firm; otherwise Report Fraud/Police Scotland and bank.

Evidence/date: FCA guidance updated 19 Jan 2026 and covers financial scams and recovery; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

12Recovery-room and refund scams [UK-RECOVERY-ROOM]

What it is: After an earlier loss, a criminal offers to recover money or buy back an investment for a fee or more data.

Warning signs: Unsolicited post-loss contact; supposed recovery agent/regulator/lawyer; advance payment; guaranteed recovery.

Guidance and reporting: FCA guidance. Do not pay; contact original bank/provider and FCA if relevant, then Report Fraud or Police Scotland.

Evidence/date: FCA explicitly warns previous victims may be retargeted; page updated 19 Jan 2026; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

13Employment and recruitment scams [UK-EMPLOYMENT-SCAM]

What it is: Fake recruiters or employers collect fees, identity documents, bank details or unpaid work through counterfeit vacancies.

Warning signs: Job offer without credible process; payment for equipment/training/checks; receive/forward-money request; look-alike domain.

Guidance and reporting: Stop! Think Fraud and NCSC checks. Report to platform/employer, bank, Report Fraud or Police Scotland; use NCSC/7726 for suspicious contact.

Evidence/date: Active identity/payment-harvesting pattern covered by current national guidance; comparable volume unavailable; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

14SIM-swap and mobile-number takeover enabled fraud [UK-SIM-SWAP-TAKEOVER]

What it is: A criminal takes control of a mobile number or authentication information, then attempts account recovery or payment fraud.

Warning signs: Unexplained loss of mobile service; SIM-change or reset notices; unsolicited 2SV codes; unknown bank/email alerts.

Guidance and reporting: NCSC online-services guidance. Contact mobile provider and bank immediately via known channels, secure email first, and report crime.

Evidence/date: Current NCSC account-security guidance supports the pattern; comparable volume unavailable; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

15Malware and ransomware-enabled theft or extortion [UK-MALWARE-RANSOMWARE]

What it is: Malicious software steals credentials/data, disrupts systems or threatens publication or harm unless demands are met.

Warning signs: Unexpected downloads; disabled security tools; mass file changes; locked systems; unusual outbound data; demand on screen.

Guidance and reporting: NCSC ransomware-resistant-backups and phishing guidance. Isolate systems without destroying evidence, activate response, notify relevant parties and report crime.

Evidence/date: Current NCSC operational guidance; checked 6 Nov 2026. Official source.

Cross-cutting defensive actions

  • Stop, break contact and verify independently; do not click or scan supplied links or QR codes.
  • Use unique passwords, a password manager, 2SV/passkeys, and current software.
  • For organisations, use independent callback and dual approval for payment or bank-detail changes.
  • Contact the bank/payment provider immediately after payment or disclosure. England, Wales and Northern Ireland: Report Fraud or 0300 123 2040. Scotland: Police Scotland 101. Emergency: 999.
  • Suspicious email: report@phishing.gov.uk. Suspicious calls/texts: 7726. Fake adverts: ASA.
