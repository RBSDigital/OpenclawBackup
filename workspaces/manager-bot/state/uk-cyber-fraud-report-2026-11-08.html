Daily UK cyber-fraud and identity-fraud awareness report

Report date: 8 November 2026 (UTC) · Last checked: 8 November 2026, 09:06 UTC · Prepared by: Ada

Executive summary. The highest-priority current UK risk is the cross-channel impersonation family: email, text, phone, adverts and fake websites used to obtain credentials, personal data or payments. NCSC’s current phishing collection records 59.6 million reports and 263.7 thousand scams removed across 466,300 URLs as of September 2026. Ofcom also lists job-offer and missed-call scams as current phone-scam themes, with the job-offer warning published 2 October 2026. Treat all ranks below as an operational awareness order, not a precise national prevalence table.

How the ranking works and limitations

Items are ranked using an ordinal triage method: 50% current official activity signal, 30% likely harm and breadth across people or organisations, and 20% recency plus corroboration across authoritative UK sources. Official datasets are not directly comparable, and several scam types are reported through overlapping channels. Labels are deduplicated: phishing, smishing and vishing are grouped where they are the same impersonation or payment theme; separate entries remain only where the lure or operating impact is materially distinct. The list is therefore a defensible current-awareness shortlist, not a claim of exact national order.

Immediate defensive actions

  • Stop, do not click, do not reply, and verify through a known official route.
  • Never disclose passwords, one-time codes, PINs or remote access; do not move money to a so-called safe account.
  • If money or bank details were involved, contact the bank immediately using the number on the card or a known website; 159 is available for participating UK banks.
  • For suspicious email use report@phishing.gov.uk; for suspicious texts and calls use 7726; report suspicious websites to NCSC.
  • In England, Wales and Northern Ireland report fraud or cyber-enabled loss to Report Fraud. In Scotland report financial crime to Police Scotland on 101. Use 999 only for immediate danger.

Ranked current activities

1. Cross-channel phishing and impersonation [PHISH-01]

What it is: Fraudulent email, text, phone call, advert or website that imitates a trusted organisation or person to obtain credentials, personal data or a payment.

Common warning signs: Unexpected contact; urgency or threat; request for passwords, one-time codes or payment; lookalike sender, number or website; pressure not to verify independently.

Reliable guidance: NCSC phishing guidance and Stop! Think Fraud reporting guidance.

How to report: Forward suspicious email to report@phishing.gov.uk; forward suspicious texts or calls to 7726; report a lost-money or hacked incident to Report Fraud in England, Wales and Northern Ireland or Police Scotland on 101; tell the bank immediately.

Sources: https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams · https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/report-scam-email · https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/report-scam-text-message · https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/report-scam-call
Publication/last-checked: NCSC: 59.6m scam reports and 263.7k scams removed across 466,300 URLs as of September 2026; checked 2026-11-08.

2. Online shopping and marketplace fraud [SHOP-01]

What it is: Fake shops, counterfeit or non-existent goods, payment diversion and seller or buyer impersonation on marketplaces and social platforms.

Common warning signs: Price far below normal; seller wants payment off-platform; newly created account; copied reviews or images; no verifiable returns or contact details; pressure to pay quickly.

Reliable guidance: Stop! Think Fraud consumer protection guidance; NCSC phishing and scam-website guidance.

How to report: Contact the marketplace, card provider or bank; report the advert to the platform and ASA where relevant; report a crime to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Sources: https://stopthinkfraud.campaign.gov.uk/ · https://stopthinkfraud.campaign.gov.uk/reporting-fraud/
Publication/last-checked: Stop! Think Fraud current campaign pages checked 2026-11-08; no directly comparable national volume published on the page.

3. Investment, pension and clone-firm scams [INV-01]

What it is: Fraudulent investment, pension, crypto or financial-service offers, including firms cloning the identity and permissions of genuine authorised firms.

Common warning signs: Unsolicited approach; guaranteed or unusually high returns; pressure to act; secrecy; requests to transfer to a new account; firm details or contact details do not match the FCA Firm Checker.

Reliable guidance: FCA Protect yourself from scams and Clone firms and individuals.

How to report: Stop contact; verify independently with the FCA Firm Checker; tell the bank; report to the FCA where relevant and to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101 after loss.

Sources: https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/protect-yourself-scams · https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/clone-firms-individuals
Publication/last-checked: FCA Protect yourself from scams last updated 2026-01-19; FCA Clone firms last updated 2025-05-14; checked 2026-11-08.

4. Business email compromise and invoice or mandate fraud [BEC-01]

What it is: Compromise or impersonation of a supplier, executive or finance contact to redirect an invoice, change bank details or authorise a fraudulent payment.

Common warning signs: Unexpected bank-detail change; urgency around payroll or invoice settlement; unusual tone or timing; request to bypass normal approval; reply-chain or address subtly changed.

Reliable guidance: NCSC phishing defence guidance and Stop! Think Fraud reporting guidance.

How to report: Pause payment; verify using a known phone number and a second approver; alert IT, finance, the bank and affected supplier; report to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Sources: https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/phishing · https://stopthinkfraud.campaign.gov.uk/reporting-fraud/
Publication/last-checked: NCSC phishing defence guidance current page dated 2026-09; Stop! Think Fraud checked 2026-11-08.

5. Account takeover and credential-reuse fraud [ATO-01]

What it is: Criminal access to email, social, shopping or financial accounts, followed by password resets, impersonation, data theft or fraudulent transactions.

Common warning signs: Unrecognised login or reset notice; changed recovery details; messages sent without permission; lockout; reused password exposed elsewhere; unexpected MFA prompts.

Reliable guidance: NCSC Recovering a hacked account and Passkeys guidance.

How to report: Use the service recovery route; change reused passwords and enable MFA or passkeys; tell the bank and employer IT; report loss or hacking to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Sources: https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/recovering-a-hacked-account · https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/passkeys
Publication/last-checked: NCSC account-recovery and passkey guidance checked 2026-11-08; passkey guidance current in 2026.

6. Authorised payment and bank-transfer scams [APP-01]

What it is: A victim is socially engineered into authorising a transfer or card payment to a criminal-controlled account, often through a false problem, opportunity or urgent request.

Common warning signs: Instruction to move money to a safe account; unfamiliar payee; urgency or secrecy; caller wants to stay on the line; payment does not fit the normal purpose.

Reliable guidance: Stop! Think Fraud recovery/reporting guidance and NCSC guidance after sharing information.

How to report: Contact the bank or payment provider immediately using a known route or 159; preserve messages; report to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Sources: https://stopthinkfraud.campaign.gov.uk/recovery-from-fraud/ · https://stopthinkfraud.campaign.gov.uk/reporting-fraud/ · https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/what-to-do
Publication/last-checked: Stop! Think Fraud reporting guidance and NCSC personal-information guidance checked 2026-11-08.

7. Romance and relationship scams [ROM-01]

What it is: A criminal builds a false personal or romantic relationship online and then seeks money, personal data, access or use of the victim’s account.

Common warning signs: Relationship moves quickly off-platform; avoids video or meeting; repeated emergencies; requests secrecy, money, crypto or identity documents; discourages advice from friends.

Reliable guidance: Stop! Think Fraud protection and recovery guidance.

How to report: Stop sending money; tell the bank or platform; preserve evidence; report to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101 and seek support from a trusted person.

Sources: https://stopthinkfraud.campaign.gov.uk/ · https://stopthinkfraud.campaign.gov.uk/recovery-from-fraud/
Publication/last-checked: Stop! Think Fraud current and recovery pages checked 2026-11-08; recovery page cites Crime Survey for England and Wales year ending September 2025.

8. Delivery, parcel and missed-delivery scams [DELIV-01]

What it is: Messages, calls or websites posing as a courier, postal service or retailer to obtain a payment, card details or personal information.

Common warning signs: Unexpected delivery fee; short-link or QR code; request to reschedule through an unfamiliar site; pressure to pay a small amount; sender or domain mismatch.

Reliable guidance: Ofcom scam calls and messages guidance and NCSC scam-text guidance.

How to report: Do not click or pay; report texts to 7726, suspicious websites to NCSC, and tell the bank if payment details were shared; report loss to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Sources: https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/scam-calls-and-messages · https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/report-scam-text-message
Publication/last-checked: Ofcom page published 2026-07-15 and last updated 2026-08-24; checked 2026-11-08.

9. Identity theft and identity-document misuse [ID-01]

What it is: Personal details or identity documents are stolen or obtained through deception and then used to open accounts, obtain services, impersonate the victim or support another fraud.

Common warning signs: Unexpected credit application or account; missing post; unfamiliar data requests; identity documents requested without a clear need; unexplained credit or fraud-prevention contact.

Reliable guidance: NCSC guidance after sharing personal information and Stop! Think Fraud protection guidance.

How to report: Contact the affected organisation and bank; change exposed credentials; consider credit-file protective steps; report to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Sources: https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/what-to-do · https://stopthinkfraud.campaign.gov.uk/protect-yourself-from-fraud/
Publication/last-checked: NCSC personal-information guidance and Stop! Think Fraud protection page checked 2026-11-08.

10. Job-offer and recruitment scams [JOB-01]

What it is: Fake recruitment messages or automated calls that seek fees, identity documents, bank details or account access under the guise of employment.

Common warning signs: Unsolicited offer; no proper interview; request for money or identity documents early; unrealistic pay; recruiter uses a personal account or unverifiable company.

Reliable guidance: Ofcom job-offer and missed-call warning and Stop! Think Fraud guidance.

How to report: Report calls or texts to 7726; report the advert or platform account; tell the bank if money or details were shared; report loss to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Sources: https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/scam-calls-and-messages/beware-of-job-offer-and-missed-call-scams · https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/scam-calls-and-messages
Publication/last-checked: Ofcom job-offer and missed-call warning published 2026-10-02; checked 2026-11-08.

11. SIM-swap and mobile-number takeover [SIM-01]

What it is: A criminal causes a victim’s mobile number to be moved or duplicated, enabling interception of calls or texts and attempted password resets or payment fraud.

Common warning signs: Sudden loss of mobile service; unexpected SIM-change message; password-reset or MFA alerts; bank activity the victim does not recognise.

Reliable guidance: Ofcom protection guidance and NCSC hacked-account guidance.

How to report: Contact the mobile provider and bank immediately via known numbers; secure accounts from a trusted device; report loss or hacking to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Sources: https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/scam-calls-and-messages/how-to-protect-yourself-from-scam-calls-texts-and-messages · https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/recovering-a-hacked-account
Publication/last-checked: Ofcom protection guidance published 2026-07-15 and last updated 2026-07-22; checked 2026-11-08.

12. Tech-support and remote-access scams [TECH-01]

What it is: A caller, advert or message falsely claims a device or account has a problem and pressures the victim to install software, disclose access information or pay for unnecessary help.

Common warning signs: Unsolicited technical warning; fake virus or refund claim; request for remote access, passwords or payment; pressure to act before checking with the real provider.

Reliable guidance: NCSC guidance after sharing information and Stop! Think Fraud recovery guidance.

How to report: End contact; uninstall untrusted software and seek trusted technical help; tell the bank and employer IT if relevant; report to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Sources: https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/what-to-do · https://stopthinkfraud.campaign.gov.uk/recovery-from-fraud/
Publication/last-checked: NCSC and Stop! Think Fraud recovery guidance checked 2026-11-08.

13. HMRC, tax, refund and benefit impersonation scams [HMRC-01]

What it is: Messages or calls impersonating HMRC or another public service to demand payment, offer a refund or obtain tax, identity or bank information.

Common warning signs: Threat of arrest or penalty; unexpected refund; request for bank details or vouchers; urgency; sender or callback route is not an official GOV.UK route.

Reliable guidance: Stop! Think Fraud reporting guidance and GOV.UK Stop! Think Fraud.

How to report: Forward HMRC phishing emails to phishing@hmrc.gov.uk; forward suspicious HMRC texts to 60599; use HMRC’s online suspicious-call form; report loss to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Sources: https://stopthinkfraud.campaign.gov.uk/reporting-fraud/ · https://www.gov.uk/stopthinkfraud
Publication/last-checked: Stop! Think Fraud reporting page checked 2026-11-08; GOV.UK link resolves to the current campaign.

14. Postal, courier-collection and doorstep payment fraud [POST-01]

What it is: Fraud using post, doorstep visits or a courier-style collection to obtain cards, cash, documents or payment under a false service or investigation story.

Common warning signs: Unexpected visitor or letter; request to hand over a card, PIN, cash or documents; insistence on secrecy; claimed official investigation; no independent verification.

Reliable guidance: Stop! Think Fraud reporting and protection guidance.

How to report: Do not hand over cards, cash or documents; contact the named organisation independently; tell the bank; report postal material to Royal Mail and a crime to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Sources: https://stopthinkfraud.campaign.gov.uk/reporting-fraud/ · https://stopthinkfraud.campaign.gov.uk/protect-yourself-from-fraud/
Publication/last-checked: Stop! Think Fraud reporting page checked 2026-11-08; page provides Royal Mail reporting route.

15. Loan-fee and advance-fee scams [LOAN-01]

What it is: A fake lender or service promises credit, compensation or recovery but demands an upfront fee or personal information and then fails to provide the promised service.

Common warning signs: Guaranteed acceptance; unsolicited offer; fee before loan; pressure to pay by unusual method; lender cannot be verified; follow-up fees after the first payment.

Reliable guidance: FCA Loan fee fraud and Protect yourself from scams.

How to report: Do not pay further fees; contact the bank; check the firm with the FCA; report to the FCA where relevant and to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Sources: https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/loan-fee-fraud · https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/protect-yourself-scams
Publication/last-checked: FCA Loan fee fraud last updated 2026-03-31; FCA Protect yourself from scams last updated 2026-01-19; checked 2026-11-08.
