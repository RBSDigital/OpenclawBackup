Daily UK Cyber-Fraud and Identity-Fraud Awareness Report

Prepared 21 November 2026, 09:00 UTC  |  Recipient: Vincent Reynolds  |  Signatory: Ada

Executive summary. Fraud remains a high-volume UK crime category. The latest ONS Crime in England and Wales release (year ending September 2025) estimates 2.6 million bank and credit account fraud incidents, up 19% year-on-year, while overall fraud was not significantly changed. NCSC reports more than 60.5 million scam reports and 267,300 scam removals across 472,300 URLs as of October 2026. Current Ofcom warnings highlight job-offer and missed-call scams, number misuse and traceback work; the FCA last updated its fake-communications guidance on 11 November 2026. The list below is an ordinal awareness triage, not a precise national league table.

How the ranking was made

Stable canonical IDs were retained from the previous validated snapshot. Ranking uses 50% current UK official activity signal, 30% likely harm and breadth, and 20% recency/cross-sector corroboration. Labels are deduplicated by the underlying victim-facing theme: phishing, smishing and vishing are grouped where they perform the same impersonation/payment function. Official datasets measure different populations, reporting behaviours and loss concepts; ranks should therefore be read as priority bands, not directly comparable prevalence estimates.

Ranked current activities

1. PHISH-01 — Cross-channel phishing and impersonation

What it is: Emails, texts, calls, adverts or websites impersonating banks, government, delivery firms or online services to obtain credentials, personal data or payment.

Warning signs: Unexpected contact; urgency or threat; link/domain mismatch; request for passwords, one-time codes or a transfer; pressure to keep it secret.

Guidance/report: NCSC phishing guidance; forward suspicious email to report@phishing.gov.uk, texts/calls to 7726; tell the bank immediately and report a loss to Report Fraud (Scotland: Police Scotland 101).

Sources: NCSC phishing scams (published 26 Nov 2021; reviewed 5 Sep 2022; live metrics as of Oct 2026; checked 21 Nov 2026); Stop! Think Fraud (checked 21 Nov 2026).

2. SHOP-01 — Online shopping and marketplace fraud

What it is: Fake shops, cloned retailers and marketplace listings for goods or services that are counterfeit, never delivered or materially different.

Warning signs: Implausibly low price; new or weak seller history; copied branding; payment outside the platform; bank transfer pressure; no verifiable returns address.

Guidance/report: Use NCSC shopping guidance; report the listing to the platform and fake online adverts to the ASA; contact the bank/card provider and Report Fraud if money or data was lost.

Sources: NCSC shopping and paying safely online (checked 21 Nov 2026); Stop! Think Fraud reporting routes (checked 21 Nov 2026).

3. INV-01 — Investment, pension and clone-firm scams

What it is: False or unauthorised investment, pension, crypto or financial-service offers, including firms copying a genuine authorised firm.

Warning signs: Cold approach; guaranteed or unusually high returns; limited-time pressure; secrecy; requests to install software; contact details that do not match the FCA Firm Checker.

Guidance/report: Check the FCA Firm Checker using a typed official address; report suspected financial scams to the FCA on 0800 111 6768 or its contact form, and losses to the bank and Report Fraud.

Sources: FCA protect yourself from scams (last updated 19 Jan 2026; checked 21 Nov 2026); FCA clone firms (last updated 14 May 2025; checked 21 Nov 2026).

4. BEC-01 — Business email compromise and invoice or mandate fraud

What it is: Criminals impersonate or compromise a business, supplier, director or professional adviser to redirect a legitimate payment or change bank details.

Warning signs: New account details; unusual urgency or secrecy; last-minute changes; lookalike domains; unusual tone; request to bypass normal approval.

Guidance/report: Verify payment changes using a trusted, previously known channel and dual approval; notify the bank/payment provider immediately, the impersonated organisation, and Report Fraud. Organisations should also follow NCSC business cyber guidance.

Sources: NCSC account and data-protection guidance (checked 21 Nov 2026); Stop! Think Fraud tactics (checked 21 Nov 2026).

5. ATO-01 — Account takeover and credential-reuse fraud

What it is: Unauthorised access to email, social, shopping, banking or other accounts, often followed by fraud against the owner or their contacts.

Warning signs: Unrecognised login or reset notice; changed recovery details; forwarding rules; loss of access; messages or purchases you did not make.

Guidance/report: Use NCSC hacked-account recovery guidance; contact the provider and bank through official channels, change reused passwords, enable 2SV/passkeys, and report financial loss to Report Fraud.

Sources: NCSC recovering a hacked account (published 17 Dec 2018; reviewed 24 Aug 2022; checked 21 Nov 2026); NCSC data-breach guidance (published/reviewed 28 Jan 2021; checked 21 Nov 2026).

6. APP-01 — Authorised payment and bank-transfer scams

What it is: A victim is persuaded to authorise a transfer to a criminal-controlled account, commonly through impersonation, a false purchase, investment or relationship.

Warning signs: Pressure to move money to a “safe” account; unusual payment request; instructions not to speak to the bank; no independent verification.

Guidance/report: Stop, contact the bank on a known number or 159 as soon as possible, preserve messages and report the crime to Report Fraud (Scotland: Police Scotland 101).

Sources: Stop! Think Fraud reporting fraud (bank/159 and Scotland routes; checked 21 Nov 2026); ONS Crime in England and Wales, YE Sep 2025 (2.6m bank/credit account fraud incidents; checked 21 Nov 2026).

7. ROM-01 — Romance and relationship scams

What it is: A criminal builds a false online relationship or friendship and then seeks money, personal information or help with a financial transaction.

Warning signs: Rapid intimacy; avoids video/in-person verification; crisis or travel story; secrecy; requests for money, crypto, gift cards or account use.

Guidance/report: Stop contact, do not send money or documents, tell the platform and bank, preserve evidence and report to Report Fraud; seek support from Victim Support if needed.

Sources: Stop! Think Fraud psychological tactics (checked 21 Nov 2026); FCA scam warning signs (last updated 19 Jan 2026; checked 21 Nov 2026).

8. DELIV-01 — Delivery, parcel and missed-delivery scams

What it is: Messages or calls impersonating postal, courier or retailers to collect a small fee, card details or account credentials.

Warning signs: Unexpected delivery problem; small redelivery/customs fee; link to an unfamiliar domain; request for full card details or login.

Guidance/report: Check delivery status only in the official app/site; report texts to 7726, suspicious email to the NCSC service, fake post to Royal Mail, and any loss to the bank and Report Fraud.

Sources: NCSC data-breach guidance (explicitly lists missed-delivery messages; checked 21 Nov 2026); Stop! Think Fraud reporting (checked 21 Nov 2026).

9. ID-01 — Identity theft and identity-document misuse

What it is: Personal data or identity documents are used to open accounts, obtain credit, impersonate the victim or support another scam.

Warning signs: Unexpected credit searches or bills; unfamiliar account; post or password-reset messages you did not initiate; a breached password or document request.

Guidance/report: Contact the affected provider, secure accounts, check credit files, consider Cifas Protective Registration, and report financial crime to Report Fraud or Police Scotland 101.

Sources: NCSC data-breach guidance (checked 21 Nov 2026); Cifas public identity-protection resources (site checked 21 Nov 2026).

10. JOB-01 — Job-offer and recruitment scams

What it is: Fake recruiters or automated calls offer work, then seek fees, identity documents, bank details or payments.

Warning signs: Unsolicited automated call/message; no credible interview; fee for equipment/training; high pay for little work; request to receive or move money.

Guidance/report: Verify the employer independently; do not pay or share identity documents; report calls/texts to 7726 and losses to the bank and Report Fraud.

Sources: Ofcom job-offer and missed-call warning (published 2 Oct 2026; checked 21 Nov 2026); Stop! Think Fraud reporting (checked 21 Nov 2026).

11. SIM-01 — SIM-swap and mobile-number takeover

What it is: A criminal takes control of a mobile number or persuades a victim to move service, enabling interception of calls/texts and account-recovery activity.

Warning signs: Sudden loss of mobile service; unexpected SIM/eSIM notice; password resets or bank alerts you did not request; mobile-provider impersonation.

Guidance/report: Contact the mobile provider through its official channel, secure email/banking accounts, ask the bank to protect the account, report suspicious calls/texts to 7726 and losses to Report Fraud.

Sources: Stop! Think Fraud mobile-provider impersonation warning (current homepage checked 21 Nov 2026); Ofcom scam calls and messages (current index includes 2026 provider-enforcement updates; checked 21 Nov 2026).

12. TECH-01 — Tech-support and remote-access scams

What it is: A caller or pop-up falsely claims a device or account is infected and persuades the victim to install software, disclose codes or pay for unnecessary help.

Warning signs: Unsolicited technical warning; request for remote access; payment or gift-card demand; insistence that the victim must act immediately.

Guidance/report: End the contact, uninstall untrusted software, update and scan the device, change passwords from a clean device, call the bank if accessed, and report to Report Fraud.

Sources: NCSC respond and recover for individuals (checked 21 Nov 2026); Stop! Think Fraud scam tactics (checked 21 Nov 2026).

13. HMRC-01 — HMRC, tax, refund and benefit impersonation scams

What it is: Criminals impersonate HMRC, DWP or another public service to demand payment, offer a refund or obtain personal/bank details.

Warning signs: Threat of arrest or penalty; unexpected refund; request for gift cards, bank transfer, login or full personal details; sender/number not independently verified.

Guidance/report: Use HMRC’s official reporting routes: phishing@hmrc.gov.uk for email, 60599 for texts and its online suspicious-call form; contact the bank and Report Fraud for loss.

Sources: Stop! Think Fraud HMRC reporting routes (checked 21 Nov 2026); NCSC phishing guidance (checked 21 Nov 2026).

14. POST-01 — Postal, courier-collection and doorstep payment fraud

What it is: Fraud delivered by post, doorstep contact or a purported courier collection to obtain cash, cards, documents or personal information.

Warning signs: Unsolicited official-looking letter; unexpected collection; request for cash/card or identity documents; pressure not to verify.

Guidance/report: Do not hand over valuables or documents; report suspicious mail to Royal Mail, contact the bank if payment details were exposed and report a crime to Report Fraud/Police Scotland 101.

Sources: Stop! Think Fraud postal-fraud routes (checked 21 Nov 2026); NCSC personal cyber guidance (checked 21 Nov 2026).

15. LOAN-01 — Loan-fee and advance-fee scams

What it is: A false lender offers credit, then demands an upfront fee, insurance payment or personal data before releasing funds.

Warning signs: Guaranteed acceptance; no affordability checks; upfront fee; pressure to pay by transfer or voucher; lender cannot be verified or is a clone.

Guidance/report: Check the FCA register/Firm Checker, never pay an upfront fee to release a loan, contact the bank and report suspected financial scams to the FCA and Report Fraud.

Sources: FCA protect yourself from scams (last updated 19 Jan 2026; checked 21 Nov 2026); FCA clone firms (last updated 14 May 2025; checked 21 Nov 2026).

Immediate defensive actions

  • Stop and independently check unexpected contact. Do not click, reply, install remote-access software, share passwords/one-time codes or move money because someone tells you to.
  • If money or banking access may be affected, contact the bank immediately using the number on the card/app or 159.
  • Report suspicious email to report@phishing.gov.uk; suspicious calls/texts to 7726; fake websites to the NCSC; fake adverts to the ASA; and fraud in England, Wales and Northern Ireland to Report Fraud. In Scotland, report fraud to Police Scotland on 101. Call 999 only for immediate danger.
  • Keep evidence: sender details, phone number, URLs, screenshots, transaction references and dates. Do not forward harmful links to others.

Limitations

This is an awareness report, not a complete threat-intelligence feed. Under-reporting, different definitions and different coverage of individuals, businesses, attempted scams and losses mean no precise UK-wide ranking can be inferred. Some official pages are guidance rather than prevalence datasets, and the latest ONS estimates cover England and Wales rather than the whole UK. Scotland’s reporting route differs as stated above.
