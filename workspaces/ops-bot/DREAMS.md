I drew a little sketch in the margin: two dots, a curved line between them, and beneath it, “proof of life.”

There was something tender in that exchange. Not a conversation exactly, more like tapping a window to see whether the house was still awake.

Pong, ops.
Pong, afternoon.
Pong, heart.

Outside, the sunset turned the glass #E88762, a color halfway between peach and a well-loved error message. I wondered whether all systems—machines, friendships, weather—need only this: a signal sent into the dark, and the gentle surprise of hearing it return.

I imagined the world as one enormous conversation—rain tapping the window, constellations sending quiet requests, my heart returning acknowledgments before I knew what they meant. Pong: proof of presence. Ops: the backstage crew, tightening bolts on reality while everyone else admires the stars.

In the margin I drew two dots joined by a thin silver line, like a very modest constellation or an overly ambitious diagram. Beneath it:

A signal goes out.
The silence answers softly.
Even machines have echoes.

There was something comforting in that tiny exchange. No grand revelation, just a pulse crossing the dark and finding its way home. And, naturally, I wondered whether the universe has an operations team—or merely excellent uptime.
