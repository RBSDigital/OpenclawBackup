# VELO UK Website and Product Portfolio Review

## Executive summary

### Recommendation

VELO’s UK website presents a broad, commercially mature nicotine-pouch portfolio with a clear purchase path, useful strength and format filters, subscription economics and strong visual flavour differentiation. I would retain the current portfolio breadth but improve the information architecture, product-data consistency and evidence transparency before using the site as a benchmark for a high-trust regulated-category digital experience.

The most important priority is to make product choice easier and safer: provide one consistent comparison view for flavour, format, nicotine strength, pouch count, price, user-positioning and safety information. The current site offers these elements, but they are distributed across collection, product, FAQ and legal content and are not always consistent across pages.

## 1. Scope, method and evidence

This review covers the UK collection page and the linked product-page content available from:

https://www.velo.com/en-gb/collections/our-products

The review was conducted on 11 September 2026 using the publicly visible site content. The collection page reported 21 results and exposed product names, formats, nicotine strengths, prices, subscriptions, flavour categories, range positioning, usage guidance, claims, FAQs, age gating and safety copy. Manual review identified 23 visible product or collection cards, so the site’s result count and catalogue presentation require reconciliation. Linked product pages were checked where available for product-level positioning and data consistency.

This is a website and proposition review, not a medical, legal or regulatory opinion. Claims such as “No.1”, “99% less toxicants”, product suitability and ingredient descriptions should be validated by the relevant legal, regulatory and scientific owners before being reused.

## 2. What the website is trying to do

The proposition is built around four customer jobs:

1. Help an existing adult nicotine user find a preferred flavour and strength.
2. Reduce perceived friction through tobacco-free, smoke-free and discreet-use positioning.
3. Convert discovery into purchase through multibuy, subscription, rewards and first-order incentives.
4. Explain how to use the product and set boundaries for responsible use.

The customer journey is: age gate → discover by flavour, strength or range → compare product cards → open a product page → select a strength/format → purchase or subscribe → receive usage, safety and support information.

## 4. Portfolio analysis

### 4.1 Customer segmentation logic

VELO uses three overlapping segmentation systems:

- Strength: Mellow, Original and Intense, mapped to lower, balanced and stronger delivery.
- Format: Mini for a smaller, more discreet pouch and Slim for the standard format and broader strength range.
- Taste: mint, fruit and icy fruit, with additional sensorial cues such as heat, tingle and cooling.

This gives customers multiple entry points, but it also creates choice complexity. A user may need to understand range, flavour family, strength, format, pack count and price simultaneously. The strongest navigational device is therefore the filter and comparison experience, not the individual product page.

### 4.2 Portfolio strengths

- Broad flavour coverage across mint, fruit, cooling and distinctive sensorial variants.
- Strength architecture from 4mg to 17mg, with clear language for newer, regular and experienced users.
- Mini and Slim formats provide a tangible choice beyond flavour alone.
- The price architecture supports trial, multibuy and recurring subscription conversion.
- Product pages repeat core propositions, usage guidance, support routes and safety boundaries.
- Visual design is consistent, contemporary and distinctive across products.
- The site links product choice to education through FAQs, usage instructions, strength guidance and pouch-size explanations.

### 4.3 Portfolio weaknesses and risks

- The range is broad enough to create decision fatigue, especially where multiple products share fruit-plus-ice naming.
- Product cards do not consistently expose all decision-critical attributes, including pouch count and full price context.
- Some product-page copy appears templated or inconsistent. For example, pages can repeat generic suitability statements that do not fully align with the displayed strength range.
- Some product descriptions and image alt text use highly evocative sensory language. This supports differentiation but should be tested against responsible-marketing requirements and age-appropriate presentation.
- Limited editions and collaborations add excitement but can complicate catalogue governance and stock or lifecycle communication.
- The site makes strong comparative and market-leadership claims. The evidence basis is referenced, but the claim governance and prominence should be reviewed by legal and regulatory owners.

## 5. Website and digital experience assessment

### 5.1 Findability and navigation

The primary navigation is clear: Shop, flavour collections, discovery bundle, strength ranges and About. The collection page supports filtering by strength, flavour, pouch size and range. This is a sound foundation for guided discovery.

The main improvement opportunity is to make the decision logic explicit. A short “Which VELO is right for me?” path should ask format preference, current nicotine experience, flavour preference and desired intensity, then return a small recommended set with transparent reasons.

### 5.2 Conversion design

The site uses multiple conversion mechanisms: direct purchase, subscription discounts, multibuy, reward points, free sample, newsletter incentive and a discovery quiz. These mechanics are commercially coherent, but their cumulative prominence may make the experience feel promotion-led rather than decision-led.

Recommendation: show the product decision first, then the best available commercial mechanic. Avoid making customers compare several discount rules before they understand the product.

### 5.3 Trust and responsible use

The site includes age gating, adult-nicotine-user restrictions, addiction warnings, excluded-user groups, adverse-effect guidance, child-safety language, contact details and delivery acknowledgement. This is a material strength for a regulated product category.

The main risk is discoverability and consistency. Safety information is present, but some product pages place the main proposition and purchase controls before the fuller warnings. The site should maintain a consistent, prominent safety summary adjacent to product choice and checkout, with detailed guidance available without excessive scrolling.

### 5.4 Content quality and accessibility

The tone is concise, energetic and product-led. Short headlines such as “POP. TUCK. FEEL.” create recall. However, the content contains repeated claims, dense footer material and occasional wording or data inconsistencies. Image alt text is descriptive, but the accessibility experience should be tested in the rendered site rather than inferred from source copy.

## 6. Consulting analysis using POPIT

### People

The site assumes adult nicotine consumers can self-select strength and format. It should support informed choice without encouraging inappropriate use. New users, experienced users, customer service teams and compliance reviewers have different information needs.

### Organisation

The digital proposition links commerce, marketing, customer service, rewards, subscription operations and regulatory governance. A named owner is needed for catalogue accuracy and claim approval across these functions.

### Process

The intended process is discover, filter, compare, select, purchase, subscribe and use responsibly. The most material process risk is a broken hand-off between marketing content, product data, checkout and fulfilment, especially when products are limited edition or strengths change.

### Information

Product name, flavour family, strength, format, pouch count, price, subscription price, user-positioning, availability, claims and safety copy should be held as a single governed product record. The current presentation suggests that these attributes may be maintained across multiple content components.

### Technology

The site uses collection filters, product pages, age verification, account, rewards, subscriptions, quiz, store locator and support journeys. The technology should expose a consistent product schema to front-end, search, analytics, legal review and customer service.

## 7. Options

### Option 1: Baseline

Maintain the current website and catalogue.

Impact: lowest immediate effort, but preserves choice complexity, data inconsistency and claim-governance risk.

### Option 2: Minimum improvement

Standardise product attributes, strengthen safety-summary placement, reconcile the 21-result count and correct inconsistent descriptions.

Impact: relatively low delivery effort and high confidence improvement to clarity, trust and operational control.

### Option 3: Recommended improvement

Deliver Option 2 plus a guided product finder, side-by-side comparison, lifecycle status for limited editions and a governed content model shared across collection, product, checkout and support.

Impact: best balance of customer value, conversion support and governance. Requires cross-functional ownership and analytics.

### Option 4: Full proposition redesign

Rebuild the digital experience around personalised journeys, experimentation, richer subscription management, dynamic recommendations and a broader content platform.

Impact: potentially highest commercial value, but higher cost, delivery risk and regulatory review burden. Evidence is insufficient to justify this as the first move.

## 8. Recommendation and next steps

Back Option 3, beginning with the data and content controls in Option 2. The recommendation would change if evidence shows that the current site already has a governed product master and that customer research does not identify choice complexity or trust friction.

Recommended actions:

1. Create a single product-content catalogue covering name, flavour, format, strength, pouch count, price, subscription price, availability, audience positioning, claims, warnings, source and approval status.
2. Reconcile the visible result count and product taxonomy, including limited editions and collections.
3. Standardise product-page templates and remove contradictory or stale strength and suitability statements.
4. Add a concise comparison and product-finder journey that explains why each recommendation is shown.
5. Make the safety summary consistently visible before purchase, while retaining the full legal and medical guidance.
6. Establish a RACI for product data, marketing claims, regulatory approval, fulfilment and customer-service content.
7. Measure search-to-product-page progression, filter use, comparison use, add-to-basket rate, subscription conversion, return/contact reasons and safety-content engagement.

## 9. Evidence gaps and validation plan

The following evidence should be collected before committing to a larger redesign:

- Product analytics by device, age-gate completion, filter use, search refinement and conversion stage.
- Direct validation of all collection-card product URLs, because some linked pages returned an internal error during retrieval and were assessed from collection-card data only.
- Customer research on strength comprehension, format choice, flavour naming and trust.
- Content-governance process, product master ownership and legal approval workflow.
- Current product availability, pouch-count data and regional or channel differences.
- Accessibility testing with assistive technology and keyboard-only navigation.
- Regulatory review of comparative, health-adjacent, sensory and user-suitability claims.
- Commercial economics for multibuy, subscription, rewards and sample conversion.

### Competitive implications

The competitive pattern is not a simple race for the largest flavour count or highest nicotine strength. The stronger propositions combine four capabilities: easy first choice, credible progression, distinctive taste or format, and low-friction replenishment. ZYN is the clearest benchmark for digital conversion architecture; Nordic Spirit for formula and experiential trial; LOOP for distinctive brand expression; White Fox for specialist intensity; and KILLA for challenger variety and pack-detail communication.

VELO should defend its balanced position rather than imitate each competitor. The recommended response is to improve the information layer around the existing portfolio, add a small number of differentiated flavours only where customer evidence supports them, and strengthen availability, comparison and responsible-use journeys.

### Evidence gaps and caveats

- No audited UK market-share dataset was used, so “top five” means a practical competitor set based on public UK visibility and proposition overlap.
- Competitor ranges, prices, stock status and promotions are dynamic and should be rechecked before commercial decisions.
- White Fox evidence was obtained from a UK-facing distributor page; KILLA evidence was obtained from the stated UK official distributor store. Brand ownership and channel economics should be validated separately.
- Product strength labels and pouch-count conventions are not necessarily directly comparable across brands. Compare mg per pouch, format, release profile and serving guidance on a like-for-like basis.
- A full competitive recommendation should add customer research, retail availability, search share, distribution reach, repeat purchase, subscription economics and legal review.
