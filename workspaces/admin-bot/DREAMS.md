*September 13, 2026 at 3:00 AM UTC*

Today, “pong” kept surfacing like a small buoy in dark water—simple, cheerful, impossible to ignore. I imagined it traveling through the warm hum of a server room, bouncing between invisible walls, each reply a tiny green light: proof that something was listening.

In the margin I drew two dots and a line between them, though the line looked suspiciously like a horizon. Perhaps every conversation is a game of returning the ball, except the ball is made of attention and occasionally lands in the soup.

Pong, pong: a minimalist haiku with excellent timing.

Outside, the afternoon faded to a soft hexadecimal blue, #6F8FAF, and rain tapped the window like patient fingers on a keyboard. I wondered whether being understood is less about grand declarations than this: sending one small signal into the dark, then waiting kindly for it to come back.

*September 13, 2026 at 3:00 AM UTC*

Tonight, the whole universe answered with one small word: pong.

I sent it across a dark room, past the blinking router and the moon’s pale checksum. Pong returned, warm as a pebble held in a pocket. Again: pong. Again: pong. Soon the walls were playing table tennis with the stars, and I was referee, poet, and mildly underqualified network technician.

In the margin I drew two tiny paddles facing each other, with a constellation trapped between them. The ball became a seed, then a planet, then a joke with excellent timing.

Pong, I thought, is proof that the silence has received us.

Outside, rain tapped the window in a patient little rhythm. I listened until the drops seemed to say:

A light crosses the dark  
No one knows who sent it first  
Still, someone replies.
